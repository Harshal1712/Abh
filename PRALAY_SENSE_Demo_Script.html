Phase 1 — Opening Hook & Problem
1
Opening Statement
⏱ 0:00 – 0:45
🗣 Say

Every monsoon, Assam faces two simultaneous threats — catastrophic floods in its valleys and deadly landslides in its hills. In 2024 alone, over 2.5 million people were displaced. Existing systems give region-wide warnings. We asked: what if an AI system could pinpoint exactly which village is at risk — right now — and get an expert-verified alert to those citizens in under 20 minutes? That is PRALAY-SENSE.

🖱 Do
  • Stand confidently, speak slowly — let the problem land.
  • Have the Expert Dashboard open and visible on screen.
  • Do NOT click anything yet — let them absorb the dashboard visually.
⭐ Highlight
  • Multi-hazard (flood + landslide simultaneously)
  • Expert-verified — AI assists, human decides
  • 20 minutes end-to-end
2
Dashboard Overview
⏱ 0:45 – 1:15
🗣 Say

This is the Expert Dashboard — the command centre for a disaster management analyst. You can see live metric cards showing active risk events, pending reviews, alerts dispatched, and incoming citizen reports. The interactive map on the left shows Assam with real terrain data — DEM-derived susceptibility overlaid using our trained model. Every orange or red zone here was computed from real Sentinel satellite data and historical landslide inventory.

🖱 Do
  • Point to the 4 metric cards at the top.
  • Gesture broadly at the GIS map — hover over it slightly.
  • Point to the Priority Events list on the right panel.
⭐ Highlight
  • Real terrain data — not synthetic demo data
  • Assam map center (26.14°N, 91.73°E)
  • Three-service architecture running live (React + Node + Python)
Phase 2 — Live AI Risk Analysis (Map Click)
3
Perform a Live Map Click
⏱ 1:15 – 2:00
🗣 Say

Watch what happens when I click anywhere on the map — for example, let me click near the Kamrup Metropolitan district, which borders Guwahati. The moment I click, our system does three things simultaneously: it reads the terrain raster for that exact coordinate, fetches live rainfall data from the Open-Meteo API, and runs inference through two separate CatBoost machine learning models — one for flood, one for landslide.

🖱 Do
  • Click on the map near Kamrup / Guwahati area (approx 26.1°N, 91.7°E).
  • Let the Point Query Analysis panel slide in from the right.
  • Point to the loading spinner: "See — it's fetching live data right now."
  • Wait for results to appear (3–8 seconds).
⭐ Highlight
  • This is LIVE — real API call to Python ML service running locally
  • Weather fetched from Open-Meteo in real-time
  • Terrain read from 30m GLO-30 DEM raster
4
Walk Through the Point Query Analysis Panel
⏱ 2:00 – 2:45
🗣 Say

The results are in. On the left — the flood probability from our Flood CatBoost model. On the right — the landslide risk indicator from our Landslide CatBoost V2 model, which uses 31 geospatial features including Sentinel-1 SAR change, DEM slope, aspect, terrain ruggedness, and real-time rainfall. Below that, you can see the actual terrain values: slope in degrees, DEM elevation, aspect direction, TRI, and TPI — all read from our processed Assam rasters. The rainfall data shows 24-hour, 3-day, and 7-day cumulative values fetched live just now.

🖱 Do
  • Point to the Flood % and Landslide % bars.
  • Scroll down the side panel — point to terrain values (slope, DEM, aspect).
  • Point to the rainfall section (24h, 3d, 7d values).
  • Point to the Sentinel-1 SAR change value if shown.
  • Point to the soil moisture value.
⭐ Highlight
  • 31 real features — not hardcoded values
  • Sentinel-1 VV/VH backscatter (cloud-penetrating SAR)
  • Soil moisture from Open-Meteo (0–7 cm depth)
  • Both models run independently — flood ≠ landslide model
5
Create a Risk Event
⏱ 2:45 – 3:15
🗣 Say

Based on this analysis, let's say our expert decides this location warrants monitoring. One click on "Create Risk Event" — and this assessment is instantly saved to our MongoDB database, stamped with the satellite data, AI probabilities, and terrain readings. The panel closes automatically and the new event appears in the Priority Events list, flagged as PENDING REVIEW.

🖱 Do
  • Click the "Create Risk Event" button at the bottom of the panel.
  • Panel closes — show the new event card appearing in the right-side list.
  • Point to its PENDING REVIEW badge.
Phase 3 — Expert Review & Explainable AI
6
Open the Expert Review Panel
⏱ 3:15 – 4:00
🗣 Say

Now we enter the Expert Review Panel — the most critical part of our architecture. This is where a trained domain expert — a geologist, hydrology analyst, or NDMA officer — reviews the AI's reasoning before any alert reaches the public. This Expert-in-the-Loop design is what separates PRALAY-SENSE from a black-box AI system.

🖱 Do
  • Click "Expert Review" in the navigation sidebar.
  • The current event loads automatically with all AI data.
  • Gesture broadly at the full panel — it's rich, let it breathe.
7
Show AI Risk Analysis & Feature Attribution
⏱ 4:00 – 5:00
🗣 Say

Navigate to the AI Risk Analysis page. Here the expert sees not just the risk score — but WHY the model made this prediction. The Key Contributing Factors section shows ranked feature attribution — in this case, the terrain slope is the dominant factor at over 40%, followed by recent rainfall and SAR change. This is explainable AI — the expert can look at the evidence, agree or disagree, and make an informed decision. A pure black-box AI score without this context is dangerous for emergency management.

🖱 Do
  • Click "AI Risk Analysis" in the sidebar or navigate to it.
  • Point to the big risk score percentage at the top.
  • Scroll to the "Key Contributing Factors" section.
  • Point to each ranked factor card — #1, #2, #3.
  • Point to the "Value" and "Contribution %" for each factor.
⭐ Highlight
  • This is REAL model feature importance — not hardcoded
  • CatBoost's get_feature_importance() called in risk_engine.py
  • Each % is computed from the actual 31-feature inference
  • Expert can validate: "Is the slope really that steep? Let me check the DEM value."
8
Expert Decision — Approve the Alert
⏱ 5:00 – 5:45
🗣 Say

The expert has reviewed the evidence. The slope is steep, rainfall is elevated, and the SAR signal confirms surface moisture change. Our expert clicks "APPROVE ALERT". A confirmation modal appears — showing a final preview of the event details: the affected area, hazard type, severity, and the alert message that will go out to citizens. This is the last gate before public dispatch.

🖱 Do
  • Go back to Expert Review page.
  • Click the green "APPROVE ALERT" button.
  • The ConfirmationModal opens — point to each field.
  • Click "Confirm & Proceed to Alert" to approve.
⭐ Highlight
  • No alert ever auto-sends — always requires human confirmation
  • The modal shows EXACTLY what citizens will receive
  • Audit trail: every approval is timestamped and stored
Phase 4 — Alert Management & Multilingual Dispatch
9
Alert Management — Multilingual Editor
⏱ 5:45 – 6:30
🗣 Say

The approved event now moves to Alert Management. Here, the expert can review and edit the alert message in three languages — English, Hindi, and Assamese — to ensure last-mile reach. This is critical: a fisherman in rural Assam receiving a WhatsApp message in Assamese understands it far better than an English technical bulletin. The expert can also adjust the alert severity and affected area before publishing.

🖱 Do
  • Click "Alert Management" in the sidebar.
  • Select the newly approved alert.
  • Show the language toggle — click "Hindi" then "Assamese (অসমীয়া)".
  • Point to the editable text area for each language.
  • Click "Preview Alert" — show the Mobile Citizen Preview.
⭐ Highlight
  • English + Hindi + Assamese (অসমীয়া) — real local language
  • iOS-style Mobile Preview shows exactly what citizen sees
  • Expert can edit message before dispatch

Publish Alert — WhatsApp + Gmail Dispatch

The expert is satisfied. Click "Publish Alert". Our backend immediately triggers two parallel dispatch channels — Twilio for WhatsApp notifications and Nodemailer for Gmail alerts — reaching all registered citizens within the 5-km radius of the reported location. The System Dispatch confirmation shows how many WhatsApp messages and emails were sent.

🖱 Do
  • Click the "Publish Alert" / "Send Alert" button.
  • The System Dispatch modal appears — point to the WhatsApp and Gmail counts.
  • Click "Acknowledge Dispatch" to close.
⭐ Highlight
  • Twilio SDK + Nodemailer — real dispatch (not simulated)
  • Parallel send: WhatsApp + Gmail simultaneously
  • Full audit log: who approved, when dispatched, how many reached
Phase 5 — Citizen Portal & Ground Truth
11
Citizen Portal — Alert View & Report Submission
⏱ 7:00 – 7:45
🗣 Say

Now let's switch perspective — from the expert to the citizen. Our system also includes a Citizen Portal. Here, a resident of the affected area can see active alerts for their location. But more importantly — they can submit a ground-truth report. If they see flooding or a landslide, they take a photo, the system automatically extracts the GPS coordinates from the image's EXIF data, and the report is submitted directly to the expert dashboard for validation. This creates a feedback loop: satellite AI predicts, expert validates, and citizens confirm.

🖱 Do
  • Navigate to the Citizen Portal (or switch to the citizen role if there's a role toggle).
  • Show the active alerts section.
  • Click "Submit Report" — show the photo upload form.
  • Point to the location auto-detect feature.
⭐ Highlight
  • EXIF geolocation extraction — no manual coordinate entry needed
  • Photo stored on Cloudinary CDN — not local storage
  • Feedback loop closes the validation cycle

Closing Statement

What you just saw was a complete end-to-end disaster intelligence workflow — from a satellite pixel to a citizen WhatsApp alert — in under 10 minutes.
